# Siddharth Fit

## Liquid Glass Fitness Portfolio — UI/UX Master Design Specification

Version: 1.0

Design direction:

**Liquid Glass × Premium Fitness × Calm Strength × Human Photography**

The website should feel like a combination of:

* premium personal-training studio
* modern Apple-inspired interface material
* calm wellness environment
* high-performance fitness brand
* editorial photography portfolio

It must NOT feel like:

* a generic gym website
* bodybuilding template
* crypto/Web3 landing page
* SaaS dashboard
* neon fitness application
* over-animated Dribbble concept

The visual language should communicate:

**Strength without aggression.
Technology without coldness.
Luxury without excess.
Motion without distraction.
Fitness without visual noise.**

---

# 1. Core Design Concept

The central visual metaphor is:

## "A calm glass interface floating over human strength."

Siddharth's real photography is the primary visual material.

The Liquid Glass layer is secondary.

The glass should frame, reveal, highlight and organize the photography.

It should never compete with Siddharth.

The hierarchy is:

1. Siddharth
2. Trust
3. Coaching philosophy
4. Proof
5. Plans
6. Contact
7. Decorative effects

Never reverse this hierarchy.

---

# 2. Liquid Glass Philosophy

Liquid Glass should not mean:

`opacity: 0.5 + blur()`

everywhere.

The visual system should simulate a physical translucent material through:

* translucency
* soft refraction
* edge highlights
* environmental tint
* layered depth
* subtle reflection
* controlled blur
* soft shadows
* dynamic lighting
* movement response

The supplied Liquid Glass reference material explicitly distinguishes modern Liquid Glass from traditional static glassmorphism: the intended material responds optically to content beneath it rather than simply applying a static blur.

For the website:

### Tier 1 — CSS Liquid Glass

Default.

Use:

* `backdrop-filter`
* translucent backgrounds
* borders
* gradients
* highlights
* shadows
* pseudo-elements

Cheap and performant.

### Tier 2 — SVG Refraction

Use selectively.

Good for:

* hero glass lens
* special buttons
* logo treatment
* decorative glass object

### Tier 3 — WebGL

Only for one optional hero/decorative object.

Never make WebGL mandatory for navigation, text, pricing or primary interaction.

# 3. Liquid Glass Layers

Use three visual depths.

## Layer 0 — Environment

Photography.

Gradients.

Ambient blobs.

Gym environment.

Background texture.

This layer provides visual energy.

## Layer 1 — Glass

Navigation.

Floating information panels.

Buttons.

Stats.

Cards.

Filters.

This is the primary interactive layer.

## Layer 2 — Focus

Important CTA.

Active navigation.

Featured achievement.

Featured plan.

Contact action.

These should have slightly stronger opacity and contrast.

Never stack five translucent layers over each other.

# 57. Typography on Glass

Critical rule:

**Text should not rely on glass transparency for contrast.**

Use a sufficiently opaque text surface when necessary.

Large text:

high contrast.

Body:

high contrast.

Metadata:

moderate contrast.

Do not put tiny gray text on translucent white glass.

The supplied Liquid Glass material repeatedly identifies low contrast and text legibility as failure modes in its references.

# 81. UX Laws Applied

The supplied Laws of UX material should explicitly inform the implementation.

## Aesthetic-Usability Effect

Premium visual quality should reinforce perceived usability.

But aesthetics cannot replace readability.

## Choice Overload

Limit primary navigation and plan options.

## Chunking

Group information into:

About

Proof

Services

Plans

Contact.

## Cognitive Load

Do not require visitors to decode the interface.

## Doherty Threshold

Interactions should feel immediate.

## Fitts's Law

Large primary CTAs.

## Hick's Law

Few important choices.

## Jakob's Law

Navigation should behave like familiar websites.

## Law of Proximity

Related information stays physically close.

## Law of Similarity

Cards and controls share visual grammar.

## Law of Prägnanz

Prefer simple layouts.

## Mental Model

Users should understand:

trainer → proof → plans → contact.

## Miller's Law

Avoid showing excessive plan features simultaneously.

## Paradox of the Active User

The website must be usable immediately without instruction.

## Peak-End Rule

Hero and footer should be especially polished.

## Selective Attention

Use contrast to guide attention.

## Serial Position Effect

Place important navigation/CTAs at the beginning and end.

## Von Restorff Effect

One primary CTA should stand apart.

## Zeigarnik Effect

Incomplete/contact journeys can retain a clear next action:

"Continue your conversation with Siddharth."

5. refraction
6. 3D

If performance suffers:

remove refraction first.

Then reduce blur.

Then reduce parallax.

Never sacrifice content.

`backdrop-filter`

is unavailable:

use opaque/semi-opaque surface.

The UI should still look intentional.

Do not make glass support a hard dependency.

# 101. Glass Fatigue Rule

Never put glass behind every element.

Target:

approximately 20–35% of visible UI surfaces using glass.

Everything else should be:

negative space.

# 109. Liquid Glass Don'ts

Do not:

blur text.

refract text.

make every card transparent.

use glass over busy images without contrast testing.

use glass solely because it looks fashionable.

use continuous specular animation.

use heavy shadows.

use rainbow glass.

# 115. UX Testing Questions

Test with real users:

2. What does he offer?

# 116. Final Design Formula

The final visual formula:

**40% human photography**

**25% negative space / solid surfaces**

**20% Liquid Glass**

**10% typography and editorial structure**

**5% motion / 3D / special effects**

This prevents the technology from overpowering the brand.
